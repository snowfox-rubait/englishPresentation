THE HOOK

You thought about it. Then you saw the ad.

Your phone isn't listening through your microphone. It’s doing something much smarter.

THE MYTH

Secret Microphones

  • Streaming audio from billions of phones is technically detectable and battery-draining.
  • Outbound packet analyses show zero evidence of secret audio transmissions.
  • The belief persists because predictive targeting feels so uncannily accurate.

THE REALITY

Predictive Modeling

  • Co-location: When you talk to friends, phones share Wi-Fi or GPS. If they search, you see the ad.
  • Behavioral models anticipate desires from demographics and scroll habits before you type.
  • Bottom line: They don't need to listen to you. They already know you.
SPEAKER NOTES (Speaker A · 45s) “Hands up if you've ever mentioned a product to a friend, or merely thought about it, and within an hour an ad for it popped up on your feed. Most people assume their phone's microphone is secretly listening. But cybersecurity packet analyses consistently show this isn't happening. Live audio streaming would drain battery, consume massive data bandwidth, and trigger privacy sensors. The truth is far more sobering: platforms don't need to listen to your voice. By correlating your location with the people around you, analyzing what your friends search for, and leveraging predictive models built from millions of users, algorithms predict what you want before you even search for it.”
