04 · INFERENCE

They guess what you never said

Not sharing doesn't protect you. Inference consistently beats disclosure.

Homophily

Peer-Group Inference

People closely resemble the company they keep. Even if you never post your political leaning, salary, or religious views, algorithms infer them with high statistical confidence based strictly on your friends' behavior.

Shadow Profiles

Non-User Tracking

Even if you have never created an account on a platform, you likely have a shadow profile. When friends sync their address books or upload tagged group photos, the platform constructs an indexed profile for you.

Behavioral Inference

Latent Trait Modeling

Models can detect early signs of depression, chronic illness, relationship strain, or job insecurity from subtle shifts in typing speed, active hours, and content consumption—without you typing a single word about it.

SPEAKER NOTES (Speaker B · 50s) “The most common defense people offer is: 'I don't post anything sensitive, so I have nothing to worry about.' This ignores how machine learning works. In data analytics, inference beats disclosure. First, homophily: you share characteristics with your peer network. If five of your friends are interested in a specific medical treatment or investment, models infer that you are too. Second, shadow profiles: platforms map non-users through address book uploads and photo metadata. You don't need an account to be tracked. Third, behavioral inference: changes in when you log on or how quickly you scroll reveal health conditions, stress levels, and life transitions long before you disclose them.”
